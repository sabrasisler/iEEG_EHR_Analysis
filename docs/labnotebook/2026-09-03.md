**Saw:**

- The loader reproduces the source analysis's independently published corpus
  totals *exactly* — 98 files, 7,340 MAR rows, 421 multi-product rows collapsed,
  6,919 unique administrations, 380 benzodiazepines, 0 unmatched drug names. That
  validates name cleaning, the dedup key and the taxonomy join in one shot against
  numbers this code did not produce. Pinned as a test.
- 1,754 analgesic administrations in 93 subjects: opioids 1,062, acetaminophen
  643, NSAIDs 49.
- **Anesthetics are effectively absent from the export.** Propofol n=1, rocuronium
  n=3, lidocaine n=21 (mostly topical), no ketamine / dexmedetomidine /
  remifentanil, and not one row with a populated `infusion_rate` across all 98
  sessions. The MAR export does not capture procedural medication. Dropped them
  from the drug set (DECISIONS 2026-09-03).
- notable: `timing_hist/*/fig2b_interdose_interval.png` — narrowing the interval
  axis from the source's 0–30 h to 0–24 h was the right call; opioid inter-dose
  intervals pile up at 4–6 h across every formulation, which was invisible spread
  over 30 h.
- notable: `hospital_day/*/fig3_hospital_day_grid.png` — administration rate
  tapers across days 0–6 for the opioids (hydrocodone-acetaminophen 2.6 → 0.7 per
  24 recorded hours, oxycodone 2.6 → 1.0) while acetaminophen stays flat at
  ~1.5. The normalized-dose row sits near 1.0 throughout for everything. Reads as
  a taper in *frequency* rather than in dose *magnitude* — nomination, not a
  finding, and it rests on a denominator that is only good to a few percent (see
  below).
- notable: `coadmin_peth/*/fig4_coadmin_peth.png` — anticonvulsant zero-bin peak
  under every analgesic row (same medication pass). In the opioid column against
  opioid rows there is a zero-bin *dip* with shoulders at ±4 h, which is the
  anchor-exclusion plus q4h redosing. IV fentanyl shows a benzodiazepine zero-bin
  peak (~0.17) that nothing else does.
- Time-of-day at the source's finer binning was noise for the low-n panels;
  moved the default to 60-min bins. Acetaminophen then shows clean medication-pass
  peaks.

**Broken / needs rerun:** The recorded-hours denominator is weaker than expected.
`sherlock_file_registry.csv` only populates `start_datetime` for runs that have a
PREPROCESSED file — all 2,136 null-timestamp rows have `has_preprocessed ==
False` — so registry timing measures preprocessed coverage, not recorded coverage.
Of the 98 med sessions, 41 are fully timestamped, 41 partial, 16 have none, and an
untimestamped run cannot be placed on a hospital day at all. Currently falling back
to MAR `session_start`→`session_end` span for 17 of the 95 analgesic sessions
(11,853 h used vs 12,759 h of span). Where the registry is complete, coverage is a
median 0.965 of span. Fig 3's rates are therefore good to a few percent, not exact.
