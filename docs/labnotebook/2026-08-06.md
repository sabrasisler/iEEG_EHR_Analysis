## BG.2 — the 1/f slope becomes a view, and its violins

`views/aperiodic.py`, `views/build_pain_epoch_slope.py`,
`analysis/plot_slope_violin.py`, `sbatch/slope_discovery_array.sbatch`,
`sbatch/plot_slope_violin.sbatch`, `tests/test_aperiodic.py`.
**Commits:** `dc3ad1a`, `18a9273`. 294 tests.

Closes the BG.2 task for the discovery split. Asked for by name: a per-epoch 1/f
slope and violins of zero / low / high, one dot per subject, all 21 `roi_v2`
regions.

### What the number is, precisely

OLS of log10(power) on log10(geometric bin centre) over the stored 50 log-spaced
bins, 1–250 Hz, minus the six flagged `contains_line_noise` → **44 bins over 2.4
decades**. Fit PER BIPOLAR CHANNEL on the epoch-mean spectrum, then slopes
averaged ARITHMETICALLY within an ROI.

**This is a broadband TILT, not a specparam exponent.** One line across 1–250 Hz
folds the low-frequency knee, the alpha/beta peaks and any high-frequency noise
floor into a single number. That is the deliberate BG.2-before-BG.3 tradeoff and
it is written into `config/psd_params.py` and both figure footnotes, so the value
does not get called an "aperiodic exponent" later by accident.

It fits better than expected: **median r² 0.970** per channel-epoch (5th pct
0.893), 0.924 at the region level. Over 2.4 decades the 1/f dominates the bumps.
r² is stored per fit and never thresholded in the build — metric/threshold split,
so `--min-r2` downstream is free.

### Three design points, each of which could have been silently wrong

- **Per channel, THEN average the slopes.** Region-averaging raw log power is
  linear-then-log, so a region "spectrum" is dominated by its loudest channel and
  fitting that would return approximately that one channel's slope wearing a
  regional label. `aperiodic.average_by_region` exists so `axes.aggregate_regions`'
  Jensen branch cannot be reached by accident — exponentiating a dimensionless
  log-log gradient is meaningless.
- **Fitting the epoch mean is free, not an approximation.** An OLS slope is a
  fixed-weight linear functional of y, so `slope(mean_t y_t) == mean_t slope(y_t)`
  exactly, whenever the windows share a finite-bin pattern. QC masking blanks
  whole windows across all bins, so within an epoch it holds. Two tests pin it;
  without them the cheap path could silently stop equalling the expensive one.
- **`normalization=none` is enforced, not defaulted.** z-scoring rescales each bin
  by its own baseline SD and therefore tilts the line by that SD's frequency
  dependence. `baseline_subtract` is a real quantity (the CHANGE in tilt) but a
  different one, and it cannot be drawn against a 0-pain violin.

### The result

Monotonic, in both units, and the ordering is the same in every summary:

| | none | low | high |
|---|---|---|---|
| native slope | −1.885 | −1.853 | −1.838 |
| within-subject z | −0.237 | +0.016 | +0.117 |

**The spectrum FLATTENS as reported pain rises.** Paired per (subject, region),
n=658: `high − none` mean **+0.361 z**, 70.4% of cells positive; `low − none`
+0.255, 64.4%; `high − low` +0.106, 61.6%. Per subject (mean over that subject's
regions), **41 of 54 subjects (75.9%)** show `high > none`.

Native effect is small — 0.047 decades/decade from none to high — which is why
the z figure is the primary and the native one is kept beside it.

No per-region breakdown is quoted here on purpose. The effect looks broad rather
than regional, and picking the best region off 21 and quoting it is exactly the
nomination-to-finding promotion `CLAUDE.md` forbids.

### Two confounds that could produce all of this, neither yet excluded

Both are logged in `SCRATCHPAD.md`; the figure means nothing until they are.

1. **State / sleep.** NREM markedly steepens the aperiodic slope. If 0-pain
   reports cluster at night or during rest — which is plausible and completely
   untested — then "0-pain is steeper" is a circadian result wearing a pain label.
   The epoch defs already carry `epoch_start_sec` and run start times
   (`qc/build_run_start_times.py`), so this is checkable without new extraction.
2. **EMG / muscle.** Broadband high-frequency power rises with muscle activity,
   which FLATTENS a 1–250 Hz line. A patient in pain tensing up would reproduce
   the entire effect. This is the same alternative that shadows the 2026-08-05
   sensorimotor high-gamma block, and the two should be examined together. A
   knee-free low-frequency fit (e.g. 1–40 Hz) would be largely immune and is one
   run away — the fit range is part of the config hash, so it lands in its own
   directory.

The `none` violin has a much wider spread than the other two (z SD 0.548 vs
0.294 / 0.316) with a long negative tail. That tail is **not** diffuse: 25 cells
below z = −1.5 over just 4 subjects, 14 of them sub-230. A single subject
appearing in 14 of 21 regions is a subject-level state effect, not anatomy —
which is itself mild evidence for confound 1.

## Scheduling note — the array shape was wrong, and it cost an hour

First submission was one-subject-per-task, copied from
`view_discovery_array.sbatch`. A slope task is ~20 SECONDS (one pass, no baseline
to accumulate), and `normal` was carrying 13,076 pending jobs: it got 16 of 60
through in 17 minutes, spending all of its time buying 20 seconds of compute per
scheduling decision.

Rewrote as chunks of 15 (`CHUNK_SIZE`, 4 tasks), one python call per subject
inside the chunk so a single bad subject costs its own line rather than its
chunk-mates' work, with a non-zero exit if any subject failed so a partial chunk
cannot read as a clean one. The last two chunks ran on `ckeller1` at Sabra's
suggestion — 6 of 24 cores were idle beside a labmate's array, so no `--qos=high_p`,
and they started immediately and finished in ~3 minutes.

**The one-subject-per-task rule still holds for the POWER view**, whose tasks are
minutes long. It is a rule about task duration, not a rule about arrays.

**Measured:** ~15 s/GB, vs ~34 s/GB for the power view — the predicted ~half, from
skipping the baseline pass.

## 2026-08-07 — the violins were the wrong figure; three replacements

Sabra: the violins make it hard to see whether subjects are increasing or
decreasing, and would a regression or a ribbon be better? Both, and the criticism
was right. `analysis/plot_slope_trajectory.py`. **Commit:** `92c7773`.

A violin has thrown the PAIRING away by the time it is drawn — the same three
violins are produced by every subject rising slightly and by half rising while
half fall. The whole effect lives in the pairing, so the pairing has to be on the
page. Three figures, one table:

- **paired** — one line per subject, none → low → high, coloured by its own
  direction, group mean ± SEM over the top. The share of subjects moving up is in
  each panel title.
- **beta** — per subject, per region, OLS of slope on the CONTINUOUS 0-10 score;
  regions on x, one dot per subject. This is the effect size + sign-consistency +
  contributing n that `CLAUDE.md` asks for, with no pooled p-value.
- **ribbon** — mean ± SEM across subjects against the continuous score,
  subject-weighted (collapse to one value per (subject, score) BEFORE averaging).

**Regression is legitimate here and the categorical worry was misplaced.**
`pain_score` is a real 0-10 report already carried per epoch; none/low/high is a
BINARIZATION on top of it (registry AXIS 7 even lists `graded` as an axis value).
Regressing on the score is declining to discretize, not imposing linearity on
categories. Measured: median 8 distinct scores and 44 epochs per subject, 54 of
56 with ≥ 3 distinct scores. Fits are WITHIN subject — a pooled fit would be
pseudo-replication and open to Simpson's paradox, since subjects differ in both
mean slope and mean reported pain.

### What the better figures changed about the conclusion

The 2026-08-06 monotonic story is **partly an artifact of the binarization**.

| | mean Δ native slope | frac > 0 |
|---|---|---|
| low − none | +0.0355 | 0.644 |
| high − low | +0.0160 | 0.616 |
| high − none | +0.0515 | 0.704 |

**Roughly ⅔ of the total effect is the none → low STEP**, ⅓ is graded above it.
The ribbon says the same and adds that above 0 the relationship is a noisy
sawtooth, not a clean ramp. Within-subject correlations are weak: median |r|
0.154. So this is a consistent-in-SIGN, small-in-MAGNITUDE effect, and the
3-level figure flattered it.

**The ribbon is the least trustworthy of the three and is annotated as such.**
Unlike the paired figure it is NOT within-subject along x: different subjects
contribute at different scores (point-wise n runs 46 down to 8), so part of the
sawtooth is composition, not dose-response. Point-wise n is drawn on the figure
for that reason.

### The sleep confound got a real test, and survived it

The 0 → any-pain step is exactly what the sleep confound predicts, because a
patient who reports a score is awake. So: refit the betas on NONZERO scores only.
`--exclude-zero-pain`.

| | median β | frac cells > 0 | frac subjects > 0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| all scores 0-10 | +0.00683 | 0.695 (n=658) | 0.759 (n=54) |
| nonzero 1-10 | +0.00478 | 0.642 (n=581) | 0.696 (n=46) |

Attenuated to ~70% of its magnitude but clearly still there. **The relationship is
graded WITHIN pain and therefore cannot be only a wake/sleep contrast.** That
weakens confound 1 substantially without eliminating it — arousal grades with
pain intensity too, and this does not separate those. EMG (confound 2) is
untouched by this check and remains wide open.
