## Citation correction, and two things it turned up

**Saw:** the decoding replication's target paper has been cited throughout this
project as "Prasad et al. 2025". **It is Huang et al. 2025** — Yuhao Huang first
author, Corey Keller last, *Naturalistic acute pain states decoded from neural
and facial dynamics*, Nat Commun 16:4371, doi 10.1038/s41467-025-59756-5. A lab
paper. Sabra caught it. Likely a misread of "Persad, Amit" in the author list.

The DOI was right everywhere the name was wrong, so no analysis is affected —
the pipeline was always built against the correct methods text. 13 occurrences
across 12 files. Fixed in place in code, sbatch headers, TASKS.md, PLANNING.md
and docs/view_registry.md; APPENDED as a correction to DECISIONS.md and left
alone in the earlier notebook entries, which are append-only.

**Two substantive findings from re-reading the methods while checking this:**

- **The paper never states its INNER fold count, and uses no leave-one-out
  anywhere.** The k = 5-or-10 rule is attached explicitly to the outer loop
  ("repeated k times as a part of the outer k-fold CV scheme"); the inner loop is
  described only as "the inner-fold CV scheme". So `arms.INNER_CV = 3` /
  `INNER_CV_LOGISTIC = 2` are OUR choices filling a gap, not a match to theirs,
  and should be described that way rather than as replication.

- **The paper DOES use blocked CV — for the facial analysis, not the neural
  one.** Verbatim: "a conservative sequential 5-fold cross-validation scheme to
  account for the temporal dependency of behavioral timepoints occurring close
  together, which could artificially increase model performance." That is exactly
  what our `blocked` scheme guards, stated by the authors, applied elsewhere in
  the same paper, and NOT applied to the self-report neural decoder. Since
  blocking roughly halves every arm here, this is worth stating explicitly in a
  write-up: it is their own standard applied consistently, not a critique from
  outside.

## Full-resolution PSD re-extraction (P1.6) + FOOOF unblocked (BG.3)

**What happened:** built a second pain-epoch base unit,
`features/pain/psd_epochs_fullres/epoch-5min-pre/`, storing the NATIVE FFT
frequency grid (0.5 Hz, 1-250 Hz, 499 bins) instead of 50 log bins. Same epochs,
same 2 s/1 s window grid, same bipolar pairs, so epoch defs, QC masks and

### The correctness gate passed bit-exactly

`features/fullres_psd_audit.py` re-bins the new output through the PRODUCTION
`log_bin_edges` + `_band_average_linear` and compares against the on-disk 50-bin
cache. **0 ulp, sub-019, 3/3 epochs.** That single comparison proves the
(n_windows+1)-second read length, the epoch offset, the bipolar pair ordering,
`detrend`, `scaling` and the log transform simultaneously.

Check B (round-trip through the stored float32 grid) measured 2.25e-06 in log10
per window, 1.04e-06 epoch-averaged. That is NOT in tension with P0.6's 2.5e-07:
P0.6 measured epoch averages, and 5.18e-06/sqrt(300) = 2.99e-07. The two agree.

### Two things that were measured rather than assumed, and both mattered

**Parquet's DEFAULT encoding was inflating the cache 49% AND causing a 16 GB
RSS.** pyarrow tries dictionary encoding on every column; on a float32 column of
~10k near-unique log-power values the dictionary is about as large as the data
plus the indices. Cohort projection went 465 GB -> 226 GB on
zstd + BYTE_STREAM_SPLIT, and sub-256's peak RSS went **16.00 GB (at the cgroup
limit) -> 1.81 GB**, 20.6 -> 10.0 GB on disk, 1346 -> 1084 s. One flag, two
problems. Verified bitwise lossless including -inf/NaN.

**HDF5 is a dead tie with Parquet on size**, so the `architecture.md` PART 1
fallback does not trigger: 0.69x vs 0.69x of the raw float32 payload on the same
sub-256 data. Unsurprising in hindsight — HDF5's `shuffle` and Parquet's
BYTE_STREAM_SPLIT are the same idea. Parquet keeps column pruning (9 frequencies
in 2 ms) and the io/sidecar machinery, so it stays.

Exact cohort size, summed over all 83 epoch_defs rather than extrapolated:
**156,905,430 rows.**

### FOOOF: two things found by running it

**fooof 1.1.1, NOT specparam.** `pip index versions specparam` returns nothing —
it has only pre-releases (2.0.0rc7), whose API has also changed. A release
candidate is the wrong dependency for a tree pinned to numpy 2.4.2 / scipy
1.16.3 / pandas 2.3.3. All verified untouched by the install.

**fooof requires EQUIDISTANT frequencies.** The first version notched 60/120 Hz
out of the knee arm's input to protect the aperiodic fit; fooof raised
`DataError: The input frequency values are not evenly spaced` and the arm never
ran. Resolved by letting fooof model the harmonics AS peaks (a 60 Hz line
artifact IS a narrow peak, and separating narrow peaks from the aperiodic
component is what the model is for) and dropping those peaks afterwards. On
sub-019: **1,579 line peaks dropped, 0 peaks surviving within 2 Hz of 60 or 120**
in either arm. The trade is that the knee arm's aperiodic fit now sees the line
noise, which is the main reason `fixed` (1-45 Hz, never meets a harmonic) is
primary.

Neither arm reaches 250 Hz, deliberately: at 500 Hz sampling that IS Nyquist, so
the top of the range is anti-alias rolloff. `SLOPE_FIT_HI_HZ = 250` inherits that
problem.

### First numbers (sub-019, 1,708 channel-epochs) — NOMINATIONS, not findings

| arm | exponent (median, IQR) | r2 | n_peaks | knee freq |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fixed 1-45 Hz | 2.109 [1.890, 2.314] | 0.996 | median 3, max 6 | — |
| knee 1-150 Hz | 3.008 [2.751, 3.767] | 0.997 | median 6, max 8 | 3.45 Hz |

Exponent positive in 100% of fits in both arms, and r2 < 0.9 in 0.0%. The
arm-to-arm divergence is expected and is the point of running both: it is the
knee plus the high-frequency floor that the existing polyfit slope folds into one
number.

**Worth a look before this is used:** the knee arm's peak count is SATURATING —
median 6 against a ceiling of 8 (max_n_peaks 6 + 2 line-noise slots), where the
fixed arm sits at 3 of 6. Over 1-150 Hz that may be crowding out real
oscillations. `max_n_peaks` is a CLI flag hashed into the view directory, so
re-running an arm with a higher ceiling is cheap — it reads the ~1.5 GB epoch-mean
view, not the ~226 GB cache. That affordance is exactly why FOOOF is a separate
job from the extraction.
